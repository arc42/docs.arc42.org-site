**Definition.** A requirement that says what the system does — a task, use case, feature,
procedure, process or user story.

**In arc42.** Section 1.1 asks for a short description of the functional requirements and the
driving forces. Five tips offer forms for them, and the choice is explicitly a trade-off rather
than a rule: activity diagrams for a visual overview with special cases, at a relatively high cost
of creation and maintenance ([[tip-1-6]]); BPMN when stakeholders find activity diagrams too
technical ([[tip-1-7]]); a numbered list as the simple and pragmatic form ([[tip-1-8]]); a
semi-formal text notation that renders to a diagram ([[tip-1-9]]); and exemplary business process
models that show a process from a stakeholder's viewpoint ([[tip-1-10]]).

**Distinguish from.** [[quality-requirement]] — a functional requirement says *what*, a quality
requirement says *how well*. arc42 separates them by place: functional requirements are
summarised in section 1.1, qualities in section 1.2 and section 10.
